This guide is for enterprise teams running a proof of concept (POC) with Fern. It covers how to structure the evaluation, who needs to be involved, and what to do at each stage. For the technical setup steps, follow the [Docs quickstart](/learn/docs/getting-started/quickstart).

<Note>
  If you get stuck at any point, reach out to your Fern account executive (AE) or solutions engineer (SE).
</Note>

## POC workflow

Complete these steps in order:

| Step | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Identify participants | Align on who owns each part of the evaluation. |
| 2. Gather assets | Collect the content and brand materials you need. |
| 3. Define success | Agree on measurable criteria before you start. |
| 4. Connect your repository | Deploy a starter site. |
| 5. Invite your team | Give participants the access they need. |
| 6. Add sample content | Test representative and complex pages. |
| 7. Publish a change | Verify your writing and review workflow. |
| 8. Apply branding | Match the site to your product. |
| 9. Test AI features | Evaluate Ask Fern answers and agent workflows. |
| 10. Review results | Compare the POC against your success criteria. |

## Before you begin

### Identify participants

| Role | Responsibility | Time needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Documentation owner | Runs the POC and completes most steps. | A few hours total |
| GitHub administrator | Approves the Fern GitHub App and connects the repository. | 15 minutes |
| Designer or brand owner | Provides logos, colors, and fonts. | 30 minutes |
| IT or identity administrator | Configures a custom domain or SSO, if tested. | 1–2 hours |

### Gather your content and brand assets

Collect the following before you start:

- Light and dark logo variants in SVG or PNG format.
- A favicon, preferably in SVG format.
- Brand colors as hex values.
- Font files or the names of your Google Fonts.
- A link to your current documentation or a content export.
- Your OpenAPI file or URL, if you document an API.
- Your 20–30 highest-traffic pages.

### Define success

Choose one or two value drivers to measure the POC against, then record the current baseline:

- **Acquisition:** Improve activation rate or time to first integration.
- **Deflection:** Reduce support ticket volume for a common topic.
- **Engineering time saved:** Reduce hours spent writing, reviewing, and maintaining docs.
- **Retention:** Increase adoption of a feature after launch.

Add two or three specific criteria you can verify during the POC. For example:

- A writer without Git experience can publish a change without help.
- Your most complex API reference page renders correctly.
- Ask Fern answers 8 of 10 common support questions and cites the correct pages.

Share the goal, baseline, and criteria with your Fern AE or SE before you begin.

## Step 1–3: Set up your site

Follow the [Docs quickstart](/learn/docs/getting-started/quickstart) to:

1. Install the Fern CLI.
2. Connect your GitHub repository and deploy a starter site.
3. Invite your team members from the [Fern Dashboard](https://dashboard.buildwithfern.com).

<Tip>
  Ask your Fern SE whether content migration is included in your POC. If it is, send them your current documentation URL, your list of sample pages, your OpenAPI file (if applicable), and your brand assets.
</Tip>

## Step 4: Add sample content

Start with your 20–30 highest-traffic pages. Include:

- Your most complex API reference page.
- A page with a large table or deeply nested list.
- A page with images, video, or diagrams.
- Two or three typical guides or tutorials.

Keep the sample under 50 pages so you can focus on migration quality rather than volume.

After migrating, compare each sample page with its source and check images, tables, code samples, internal links, and navigation placement. Send any migration issues to your Fern SE in one list with the page URL and the expected result.

## Step 5: Publish a change

Ask a writer who does not use Git to complete this step.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a branch">
    Open the [Fern visual editor](https://dashboard.buildwithfern.com). Click the branch selector and create a new branch to keep the draft separate from your live site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit a page">
    Make a small change in the visual editor: update a heading, add a sentence, or insert a component.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate a preview">
    Run `fern generate --docs --preview` to generate a shareable preview URL. Send it to a reviewer and make any requested changes on the same branch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish">
    Run `fern generate --docs` to publish the change to your live site. Confirm the change appears at your docs URL.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 6: Apply branding

Configure your brand in `docs.yml`:

```yml docs.yml
colors:
  accent-primary:
    dark: "#YOUR_DARK_COLOR"
    light: "#YOUR_LIGHT_COLOR"

logo:
  dark: docs/assets/logo-dark.svg
  light: docs/assets/logo-light.svg
  height: 40

favicon: docs/assets/favicon.svg
```

Check that links and buttons remain legible in both light and dark mode. See [Site settings](/learn/docs/configuration/site-level-settings) for fonts, SEO, and layout options.

If you would rather not edit the config yourself, send your assets to your Fern SE.

## Step 7: Test AI features

Fern's [Ask Fern](/learn/docs/ai-features/ask-fern/overview) feature answers reader questions using your published content.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enable Ask Fern">
    Turn on Ask Fern from the [Fern Dashboard](https://dashboard.buildwithfern.com) for your docs site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test real questions">
    Collect 20–30 recent questions from support tickets or community channels. Ask each one through Ask Fern on your site and record whether the answer is correct and cites the right page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review gaps">
    Use wrong or missing answers to identify pages to update or create. If a relevant page is not cited, make its frontmatter `description` more specific.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 8: Review results

Book one hour with your decision maker. Start with the goal and baseline you defined before the POC, then review:

| Criterion | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| A non-developer can publish | The step 5 result and time required. |
| Engineers keep review control | The pull request and its checks. |
| Complex content migrates correctly | Side-by-side sample pages. |
| Ask Fern answers accurately | Your scored questions and cited pages. |
| Content gaps are identifiable | Unanswered questions and reader feedback in the Dashboard. |

Share the results with your Fern AE or SE. They can help you interpret the data and plan next steps.

## Suggested timeline

| Week | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Set up the site, invite the team, migrate sample content. |
| Week 2 | Publish changes, apply branding, test Ask Fern. |
| Week 3 | Review results with the decision maker. |
